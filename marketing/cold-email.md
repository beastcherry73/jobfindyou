# Cold email templates

Drafts only. **Nothing here has been sent.** Read each one, change anything
that does not sound like you, and send from your own address, in small
batches, one at a time.

## Rules baked into these drafts

- They say who you are: a student who built the tool. No implied job title, no
  invented company, no "our team".
- No invented numbers, no testimonials, no claimed placement results.
- One ask per email, and the ask is small (look at it / a 15-minute call).
- Nothing is attached. Attachments from strangers do not get opened.
- Plain text. HTML signatures and logos make a personal email look like a
  mailshot.

## Before sending

1. Find the real name. "Dear Sir/Madam" reads as bulk.
2. Look up one true, specific thing about that college or institute and put it
   in the first line. If you cannot find one, do not send to them.
3. Subject lines stay under 60 characters and promise exactly what the email
   contains.
4. Send Tuesday–Thursday, 10am–noon. Not Monday, not Friday evening.
5. Track who you sent to, when, and what happened, in a sheet. Never send the
   same person the same email twice.

## Replies you should expect, and honest answers

**"Is it really free? What's the catch?"**
> It's free. There are paid plans listed on the site for features that don't
> exist yet, and they're marked "coming soon" — nothing today is behind a
> payment, and there's no card required.

**"Who are you / which company is this?"**
> No company. I'm a student, I built it myself, and it runs on free hosting.

**"How is this different from {other tool}?"**
> Honest answer: there's a comparison page at
> https://www.jobspike.in/compare/resumax that lists where the other tool is
> ahead too. The two things I'd point to: it's free with no monthly caps, and
> every job on it links to the employer's own application page.

**"What do you do with our students' resumes?"**
> Resumes are stored privately against that person's account so they can see
> their history, and they can use the tool without an account at all. It is
> not sold or shared. (Do not promise more than this, and if you change
> anything about storage, change this answer.)

**"Can you come and do a session for our students?"**
> Say yes only if you actually can, and keep it to a 20-minute live
> walkthrough. Do not promise recordings, certificates or placement help.
