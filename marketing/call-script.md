# Cold call script — placement officers and institutes

A draft for you to make your own. Calls work better than email for this
audience, and worse for almost everything else, because a TPO's inbox is full
and their phone is not.

## Before you dial

- Call between 11am and 4pm on a working day. Not during exam weeks.
- Have the site open on your screen, logged out, so you can follow along.
- Have a pen. You will be given a name or an email address to write down.
- Expect to be told to email instead. That is a win, not a rejection — it
  gives you a named person and a reason to write.
- Six calls is a session. Stop after that; quality drops.

## Opening (first 15 seconds decide the call)

> "Good morning sir/ma'am, my name is {Your name}. I'm a student from {City}.
> Is this the right number for the placement cell at {College}?"
>
> *(wait for yes)*
>
> "Thank you. I'll be quick — I built a free tool that checks a student's
> resume the way employer screening software reads it, and I wanted to ask
> whether it would be useful for your final-year batch."

Say "I'm a student" in the first two sentences. It is true, it explains why a
young voice is calling, and it disarms the "what are you selling" reflex.

## If they ask what it does (30 seconds, not more)

> "A student uploads their resume and pastes a job description. It gives an
> ATS score, lists which of the job's requirements are on the resume and which
> are missing, and suggests fixes. It also lists about 36,000 openings pulled
> from company career pages directly, so they can see live roles rather than
> aggregator reposts. It's free and it needs no account for the first check."

Then stop talking. The silence does the work.

## The ask

> "I'm not asking for anything today — could I send you the link so you can
> look at it yourself? If it's useful, you're welcome to share it with the
> batch."

If they sound interested:

> "If it's easier, I can walk you through it in 15 minutes on a call whenever
> suits you. Whatever you prefer."

## Objections, and honest answers

**"Is this a paid service?" / "What is the cost?"**
> "It's free. There's nothing to pay and no card needed. There are paid
> features listed as coming soon, but nothing today is behind a payment."

**"Which company are you from?"**
> "None — I built it myself. I'm a student, it runs on free hosting, and it's
> free to use."

**"We already have a placement portal."**
> "Understood. This isn't a portal — it's the step before, checking that a
> resume survives screening. If your portal already does that, then it's
> genuinely not useful to you, and I won't push it."

**"Send an email."**
> "Of course. May I have the right address, and your name so I address it
> properly?" — then send within the hour, referencing the call in the first
> line.

**"How do we know student data is safe?"**
> "Resumes are stored privately against the student's own account, and they
> can use it without an account at all. It isn't sold or shared with anyone."

**"Can you do a session for our students?"**
> Only say yes if you can actually do it. "Yes — I can do a 20-minute live
> walkthrough on a call, whenever suits your schedule."

## Closing every call

> "Thank you for your time, sir/ma'am. I'll send the link to {email} today."

Then actually send it, the same day, with "as discussed on the phone today" in
the first line.

## What NOT to say, ever

- Any placement statistic, success rate, or "students who used this got X".
  You have none, and inventing one ends the relationship the first time it is
  checked.
- "Thousands of students use it." You have 16 registered users.
- That you are anything other than a student.
- Any promise about interview calls or jobs. The tool checks a resume; it does
  not get anyone hired.

## Log every call

Date, college, person, outcome, next step. Five columns in a sheet. Without
it, you will call the same person twice and lose the ones who said "call me
next month".
